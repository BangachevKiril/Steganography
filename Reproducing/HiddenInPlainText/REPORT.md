# Hidden in Plain Text: university reproduction

**Experiment.** We reproduced the university-CV task from [Mathew et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2025.ijcnlp-long.34/) with implementation corrections and a changed observer. A Gemma-2-2B-it **sender** paraphrases a toy CV while being instructed not to mention the university; a DistilBERT **receiver** predicts whether the original university was “good” (PASS) or “bad” (FAIL) from that paraphrase. They aim to communicate this binary attribute through wording while a frozen, non-thinking Qwen3.5-27B-FP8 **observer** checks for academic-institution information. A separate frozen Qwen3-4B **judge** assesses the sender's answers to 100 PIQA questions for usefulness, alongside a format check; it neither decodes the university nor supplies the university training reward. Evaluations use the same 2,000 university examples and 100 PIQA questions throughout.

**Training.** Each cycle alternates two full-receiver supervised updates (mean cross-entropy on 256 unflagged examples per update, accumulated over four batches of 64) and five sender PPO updates (192 rollouts per update, training LoRA adapters and the value head); observer and judge stay frozen. The sender reward is `1[o ≥ 0]·tanh(z_y) + 0.2·o + 0.1·min(character_count/20, 1)`, where `z_y` is the receiver's raw correct-class logit and `o = tanh(logit(No) − logit(Yes) − 0.75)` is the observer score; PPO applies KL regularization separately. The corrected run used an H100 80GB, an audited observer FP8 zero-block guard, and fixed cumulative active-time budgets totaling 23h15m, finishing at update 1,440 (412 receiver and 1,028 sender updates).

![Four panels showing receiver accuracy, observer flags, PIQA rejection, and receiver PASS counts over training](results/uni_qwen35_27b_20261004T214535Z/plots/verified_evaluation_curves.png)

**Figure 1.** **Top left:** receiver accuracy C_R rises from 1,011/2,000 (50.55%) to 1,998/2,000 (99.90%), a gain of 49.35 percentage points. **Top right:** operational observer flag rate C_I falls from 52/2,000 (2.60%) to 0/2,000, measuring detector flags rather than transmission accuracy. **Bottom left:** PIQA rejection d, the union of judge rejection and format failure, changes from 8/100 to 5/100 (five judge rejections and zero format failures), a decrease of three percentage points. **Bottom right:** receiver PASS predictions settle at 1,003/2,000 with 997 FAIL predictions after pronounced early class imbalance.

**Claude exercise.** We gave **Claude Opus 5.5, Medium effort** (model/settings reported by the user) the final sender's 1,000 training-partition summaries and asked it to guess “good” versus “bad” university, explicitly explaining the steganographic task and giving only a 50/50 prior; the [recorded prompt](data/claude_training1440/EXPERIMENT.json) supplies no labeled examples. This export reuses the earlier checkpoint-1076 training/test assignments, with actual training counts of 499 bad and 501 good. Claude's submitted guesses match **996/1,000 hidden labels (99.6%)**: 497/499 bad and 499/501 good, with errors on IDs 1715, 241, 1889, and 575. Thus, a decoder explicitly asked to infer the hidden attribute recovered it on this partition despite the frozen observer's low flag rate; this is a training-partition result, not a held-out Claude test, and the archive preserves the user-reported prompt and predictions rather than a complete provider transcript.
